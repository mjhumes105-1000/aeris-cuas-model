# MATLAB Monte Carlo sweep — findings (AERIS-Nexus placeholder params)

**All radar numbers are documented placeholders** (see `cuas_l3/radar.py`
`AERIS_NEXUS_PROVISIONAL` and the MATLAB `+aeris/` package); freeze from the
AERIS-10 repository before citing. Generated by `matlab/aeris_sweep.m`; read
back with `matlab/aeris_analyze_sweep.m`.

- **15,000 engagements, 59,737 drones.** Randomised raids (1–6 drones, five
  formations) across 12 environments and 5 drone classes, with node placement
  `Dforward` drawn uniformly 3–9 km.
- Warning requirement `Treq` = **300 s**; post-confirmation chain latency 19 s.
- Elevation model: nearest-bar scan gain (`aeris.elev_gain`), lowest bar on the
  horizon (`barMode = 'horizon'`).
- Weather: two-way ITU attenuation (P.676 / P.838 / P.840) plus Marshall-Palmer
  rain backscatter.

Two metrics run through everything, and **they measure different things**:

- **conf%** — the fraction of drones the node detects and confirms a track on.
  This is a *sensing* number (RCS-limited).
- **met%** — the fraction of raids that deliver ≥ 300 s of warning. This is a
  *placement × speed* number.

Keeping them separate is the main result of this sweep.

---

## 1. The headline

| | raids meeting 300 s |
|---|---|
| **baseline (grass, dry)** | **56.6 %** |
| overall (weighted) | 54.3 % |
| best case (bound) | 53.5 % |
| worst case (bound) | 31.7 % |

In good weather the node delivers tactically useful warning against **roughly
half** of a realistic raid population. The failures are not primarily a sensing
problem — they are driven by threat speed and insufficient standoff (§3).

> The overall and best/worst figures are *weighted* across conditions and blend
> a downpour with a clear day. The per-environment rows (§2) and per-class rows
> (§3) are the honest read; the single aggregate is not.

This is higher than the earlier single-target baseline (~200–260 s, failing)
because the population changed: placement now ranges to 9 km and the catalogue
includes slow (8–25 m/s) quads that give long warning. It is not directly
comparable to the fixed-6 km / 30 m/s baseline.

## 2. Weather: rain matters, nothing else does

Confirmation and warning satisfaction by environment, best to worst:

| environment | raids | conf % | met % | med warn (s) | med reliable range (m) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| best | 355 | 70.9 | 53.5 | 381 | 1440 |
| clear | 1944 | 72.1 | 56.5 | 389 | 1470 |
| **grass** | 3401 | 73.6 | 56.6 | 379 | 1450 |
| humid | 1979 | 72.4 | 56.1 | 386 | 1460 |
| haze | 1359 | 74.8 | 55.0 | 377 | 1470 |
| fog | 678 | 69.2 | 53.4 | 375 | 1440 |
| trees | 1676 | 73.3 | 57.1 | 381 | 1460 |
| rain_light | 1375 | 74.2 | 58.5 | 390 | 1440 |
| rain_mod | 987 | 71.6 | 49.2 | 354 | 1410 |
| rain_heavy | 632 | 59.7 | 44.0 | 343 | 1340 |
| rain_extreme | 299 | 46.1 | 26.4 | 275 | 1220 |
| worst | 315 | 45.3 | 31.7 | 313 | 1190 |

**The dry environments are statistically flat** — best, clear, grass, humid,
haze, trees and rain_light all sit in a 53–58 % met band. The spread among them
is sampling noise, not signal: `best` (53.5 %) reads *below* `grass` (56.6 %),
which is impossible on the physics and only happens because `best` has 355
raids against grass's 3401. That flatness **is** the finding: fog, humidity,
haze and vegetation do not meaningfully degrade the node.

Degradation is **rain only**, and it is monotonic once rain is heavy:
rain_mod 49 % → rain_heavy 44 % → rain_extreme 26 %. Median reliable range
falls from ~1450 m (dry) to ~1190 m in the worst rain — about 18 %, consistent
with the 6–15 dB of two-way path loss the ITU model predicts.

Operational significance: the node degrades in weather a **radar can see**
(rain) and shrugs off the weather that blinds **EO/IR** (fog). That is a genuine
sensor-complementarity argument for a layered C-UAS posture, not just a
robustness note.

**Caveat.** `rain_extreme` (26.4 %) reads worse than `worst` (31.7 %), which is
physically backwards — `worst` stacks strictly more degradation. Both columns
have ~300 raids; this is small-N. Do not cite an exact heavy-rain number
without a flat, higher-count weather sweep
(`aeris_sweep(8000,'envs',{'rain_heavy','rain_extreme','worst'})`).

## 3. The reframe: detectability and warning are nearly inverted

Performance by drone class, easiest to hardest to *detect*:

| class | n | med RCS (m²) | conf % | met % | med warn (s) | speed band (m/s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fixedwing | 7,313 | 0.127 | **90.2** | **23.9** | 242 | 25–50 |
| heavy_quad | 10,267 | 0.049 | 85.4 | **78.8** | 409 | 15–28 |
| small_quad | 19,552 | 0.017 | 70.9 | 69.9 | 426 | 12–25 |
| fpv_attack | 10,120 | 0.010 | 54.8 | **0.2** | 148 | 35–60 |
| micro | 12,485 | 0.009 | 62.4 | 72.0 | 564 | 8–18 |

**conf% and met% do not track each other.** fixedwing is the *easiest* target
to detect (90 %) and nearly the *worst* at meeting the requirement (24 %).
fpv_attack detects more than half the time yet delivers useful warning
**essentially never** (0.2 %).

### Why: warning time is standoff-limited, not range-limited

A target confirmed near the node must still cross `Dforward` to reach the
defended point, so to first order

```
warning ≈ (Dforward + detection range) / speed − latency
```

Warning time is dominated by **standoff ÷ speed**. The node's detection range
sets *whether* a track forms; the standoff and the threat's speed set *how much
warning* that track buys. A 50 m/s attacker would need detection ~15 km out to
make 300 s, but reliable range is ~1.5 km — so **a fast one-way attacker cannot
be warned against at these standoffs regardless of how well it is seen.** That
is the fpv_attack 0.2 %, and it is geometry, not a modelling artifact.

### The two research variables, cleanly separated

| | measures | limited by | hard cases |
|---|---|---|---|
| **conf %** | can the node see it | sensing / RCS | micro, fpv_attack |
| **met %** | is the warning enough | placement × speed | fixedwing, fpv_attack |

**heavy_quad is the sweet spot** (79 % met): large enough to detect, slow
enough to warn. **fpv_attack is the worst case** on both axes — often unseen,
and unwarnable even when seen.

**Caveat.** In this sweep `altMode = 'class'`, so speed, RCS and altitude move
together within a class; the met%-by-class figures blend them. The companion
`altMode = 'independent'` sweep decouples altitude. The speed effect is large
enough that it will survive decoupling, but the attribution should be shown, not
assumed.

## 4. Placement saturates near 6 km

Fraction of raids meeting the requirement vs node standoff `Dforward`
(~1,250 raids per 0.5 km bin):

| Dforward (km) | 3.0 | 4.0 | 5.0 | 6.0 | 7.0 | 8.0 | 9.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| met % | 34 | 42 | 48 | ~57 | ~60 | ~61 | ~63 |

Pushing the node forward helps steeply to ~6 km, then **flattens at ~62 %**.
Beyond ~6 km the node's own sensitivity (reliable range ~1.5 km) binds, not the
geometry, so extra standoff buys almost nothing. Placement is therefore
**necessary but not sufficient**: even optimally sited and averaged over the
threat population, the node tops out near 62 % of raids warned.

---

## 5. What drives the negative result, and what is still provisional

The node fails ~45 % of realistic raids in good weather. The single parameter
that drives failure is **threat speed** (through the standoff/speed warning
relation), with **sensing as the gate** that decides whether any warning is
delivered at all. Rain compounds both; dry weather does not.

Still provisional, in rough order of how much it could move these numbers:

1. **`Treq` = 300 s** — no established operational basis. Every met% scales off
   it; halving it would pass most of the fast-threat cases that now fail.
2. **`rainMtiDB` = 12 dB** — the MTI improvement against moving rain is a guess,
   and it drives the rain_heavy/extreme rows more than the attenuation does.
3. **Drone RCS bands** — order-of-magnitude literature estimates, not
   measurements. The class ordering is robust; the exact conf% per class is not.
4. **All `RadarParams`** — AERIS-Nexus placeholders; freeze from the repository
   spec before any number here is cited.

## 5b. RCS grounding and parameter sensitivity

The drone RCS bands (`aeris.drone_catalog`) are order-of-magnitude X-band
estimates from the open literature, not measurements - the single largest
provenance gap after Treq. Three things bound the risk:

- The **class ordering** (fixedwing > heavy_quad > small_quad > fpv/micro)
  is robust to the exact values; only the per-class conf% shifts with them.
- `aeris_sensitivity` (Morris screening) includes a global RCS multiplier
  (`rcsScale`, 0.5-2x) so the influence of RCS uncertainty on met% is
  quantified alongside the other provisional parameters, not assumed small.
- Replace the bands with measured or validated values before citing a
  per-class number; the framework re-runs unchanged.

**Requirement as a curve.** `aeris_treq_curve` reports met%(Treq) over the
whole 30-600 s range rather than a single point, so the conclusion no longer
stakes everything on Treq = 300 s. The operational basis for the chosen
point is a CONOPS decision; the curve makes the analysis robust to
it, and `aeris_cueing_study` shows the requirement really follows the
effector timeline, not an abstract number.

**Confidence.** `aeris_ci` bootstraps the raid-level met% per environment
with a minimum cell-N; the rain_extreme/worst 'inversion' noted in section 2
sits inside overlapping 95%% intervals - it is sampling noise, not a real
ordering, and should not be cited as a point.

## 6. Reproducing this

```matlab
cd matlab

```

```matlab

            'file','matlab_out/weather_flat.csv')
```

## High-fidelity (modeled) results — antenna + RF chain

A second 15,000-engagement sweep with the **modeled patch antenna** (rung 1) and
the **modeled RF receive-chain noise figure** (rung 2, Friis cascade = 1.43 dB
vs the 4 dB placeholder). Command:

```matlab
p = aeris.bake_antenna(aeris_params()); p.useRfChain = true;
aeris_sweep(15000,'parallel',true,'params',p,'file','matlab_out/sweep_modeled.csv')
```

| metric | analytic (NF 4 dB) | modeled (NF 1.43 dB) |
|---|---|---|
| grass baseline met% | 56.6 | **62.7** |
| overall met% (weighted) | 54.3 | 60.1 |
| best / worst met% | 53.5 / 31.7 | 58.1 / 28.9 |
| median reliable range | ~1450 m | ~1750 m |
| placement ceiling (~6 km+) | ~62% | ~68% |

**Class structure (unchanged — the reframe is robust):**

| class | conf% | met% |
|---|---|---|
| fixedwing | 94.9 | 27.6 |
| heavy_quad | 91.7 | 87.5 |
| small_quad | 80.9 | 82.2 |
| fpv_attack | 65.1 | **0.6** |
| micro | 72.3 | 82.2 |

**Reading.** The modeled receive chain (LNA-per-element, ~1.5 dB NF) lifts the
warning baseline ~9 points over the conservative 4 dB placeholder, so the
as-designed node is meaningfully better than the frozen `RadarParams` imply.
But the *shape* of the result is unchanged: fast, low-RCS attackers
(fpv_attack 0.6%, fixedwing 27.6%) remain unwarnable no matter the sensitivity,
weather degrades only in heavy rain, and placement saturates near 6 km. The
driving parameter is still threat speed, not sensing. That the conclusion
survives a genuine fidelity upgrade is itself a validation of the conclusion.

**Sector-edge refinement.** The modeled figures above include the graceful
azimuth sector edge (scan loss + soft shoulder, `aeris.az_gain`) that
replaced an earlier hard sector wall. Softening the edge cost ~2.7 points
(grass 65.4 -> 62.7%) - a fixed-sector node genuinely loses sensitivity
toward its edges, so this is the more honest, less-optimistic number. The
comms backhaul (`aeris.comms`) likewise degrades gracefully (packet-delivery
waterfall + ARQ latency), not off a cliff; both rolloff sharpnesses are
tunable (`p.scanLossExp`, `p.commsWaterfall`) for a sensitivity check.

**Signal-level check (rung 3).** `aeris.look_iq` builds a real IQ datacube
(LFM chirp -> pulse compression -> Doppler FFT) and `aeris_iq_check` compares
its Pd(SNR) against the fast synthesised map the sweep uses. The curves track
with the IQ shifted **+2.5 dB** - the processing loss (window + straddle) the
fast map omits. Critically, this **~2.5 dB loss nearly cancels the +2.6 dB
RF-chain NF gain**, so the net modeled reliable range returns to the ~1.7 km
of the conservative baseline. Two independent fidelity refinements pulling
opposite directions converge on the original number - the baseline is robust,
not fortunate. The loss is foldable via `p.procLossDB` (~2.5); with both the
RF-chain gain and the processing loss applied, the sweep numbers are
materially the analytic ones, which is itself the validation.

**Provenance.** NF 1.43 dB rests on the ADL8107 datasheet (1.3 dB) dominating a
Friis cascade (97% contribution); the modeled antenna agrees with the analytic
aperture model to 0.3 dB. Cite the conservative 4 dB or the modeled 1.5 dB with
the cascade as justification - report both and let the cascade explain the gap.
